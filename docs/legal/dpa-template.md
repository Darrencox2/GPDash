## 1. Parties and definitions

### 1.1 The parties

This Data Processing Agreement (**"DPA"**) is made between:

(1) **\[Practice legal name\]** of \[Practice registered address\], being
    a provider of NHS general medical services (**"the Controller"**); and

(2) **\[GPDash legal entity name\]** of \[GPDash registered address\],
    trading as GPDash (**"the Processor"**, **"we"**, **"us"**).

Each a **"Party"** and together the **"Parties"**.

### 1.2 Background

(A) The Controller has agreed to use the Processor's GPDash software-
    as-a-service platform (the **"Service"**) for the purpose of
    administering its general medical practice operations, including
    appointment slot capacity tracking, clinician working patterns,
    rota management, and related practice operational data.

(B) In the course of providing the Service, the Processor will process
    personal data on behalf of the Controller.

(C) This DPA sets out the terms on which the Processor will process
    that personal data, and forms part of the agreement between the
    Parties for use of the Service (the **"Principal Agreement"**).

### 1.3 Definitions

In this DPA:

- **"Personal Data"** has the meaning given in the UK GDPR;
- **"Processing"** has the meaning given in the UK GDPR;
- **"Sub-processor"** means any third party engaged by the Processor
  to process Personal Data on behalf of the Controller;
- **"Data Subject"** has the meaning given in the UK GDPR;
- **"UK GDPR"** means the United Kingdom General Data Protection
  Regulation as defined in the Data Protection Act 2018;
- **"Applicable Data Protection Law"** means the UK GDPR, the Data
  Protection Act 2018, and any other UK or EU law that applies from
  time to time to the Processing of Personal Data;
- **"Personal Data Breach"** has the meaning given in the UK GDPR.

3.1 The Parties acknowledge that, for the Processing described in
    Schedule 1, the Controller is the controller and the Processor

## 4. Processor obligations

The Processor shall:

### 4.1 Documented instructions

Process the Personal Data only on documented instructions from the
Controller, including with regard to transfers of Personal Data outside
the United Kingdom. The Controller's instructions are set out in this
DPA, the Principal Agreement, and any configuration choices the
Controller makes within the Service.

If the Processor is required by UK or EU law to process Personal Data
other than on the Controller's documented instructions, the Processor
will inform the Controller of that legal requirement before processing
(unless the law prohibits such information on important grounds of
public interest).

### 4.2 Confidentiality

Ensure that any natural persons authorised by the Processor to process
the Personal Data have committed themselves to confidentiality or are
under an appropriate statutory obligation of confidentiality.

### 4.3 Security

Implement and maintain appropriate technical and organisational measures
to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, as set out in
**Schedule 2: Technical and Organisational Measures**.

### 4.4 Sub-processors

(a) The Controller provides general written authorisation for the
    Processor to engage the Sub-processors listed in
    **Schedule 3: Sub-processors** as at the date of this DPA.

(b) The Processor shall inform the Controller of any intended changes
    concerning the addition or replacement of Sub-processors no fewer
    than 30 days before the change takes effect, giving the Controller
    the opportunity to object.

(c) If the Controller objects to a proposed change of Sub-processor on
    reasonable data protection grounds, the Parties shall discuss in
    good faith. If no resolution can be reached, the Controller may
    terminate this DPA and the Principal Agreement insofar as it
    relates to Processing requiring the proposed Sub-processor, and
    the Processor shall refund any pre-paid fees for the unused portion
    of the term.

(d) The Processor shall enter into a written agreement with each
    Sub-processor that imposes substantially the same data protection
    obligations on the Sub-processor as those imposed on the Processor
    under this DPA.

### 4.5 Assistance with Data Subject rights

Taking into account the nature of the Processing, the Processor shall
assist the Controller by appropriate technical and organisational
measures, insofar as possible, for the fulfilment of the Controller's
obligation to respond to requests by Data Subjects exercising their
rights under UK GDPR Chapter III.

The Service provides built-in self-service mechanisms for the principal
rights (access, rectification, erasure, portability) — these are
described in the Processor's public privacy notice. Where a Data Subject
contacts the Controller rather than the Processor, the Controller may
direct the Data Subject to use the self-service mechanism or may forward
the request to the Processor for handling, and the Processor will
respond within reasonable time limits.

### 4.6 Assistance with security and breach obligations

Taking into account the nature of Processing and the information
available to the Processor, assist the Controller in ensuring compliance
with the Controller's obligations under UK GDPR Articles 32 to 36
(security of processing, breach notification, breach communication, and
data protection impact assessments).

### 4.7 Personal Data Breach notification

(a) The Processor shall notify the Controller without undue delay, and
    in any event within 48 hours, of becoming aware of a Personal Data
    Breach affecting the Controller's Personal Data.

(b) The notification shall include, to the extent then known:
    - The nature of the Breach
    - The categories and approximate number of Data Subjects concerned
    - The categories and approximate number of records concerned
    - The likely consequences of the Breach
    - Measures taken or proposed to be taken to address the Breach,
      including mitigation
    - Contact details for further information

(c) The Processor shall cooperate with the Controller and provide
    reasonable additional information as it becomes available, to help
    the Controller meet its own obligation to notify the ICO within
    72 hours where applicable.

### 4.8 Return or deletion of data

On termination of the Principal Agreement, the Processor shall, at
the choice of the Controller, return or delete all Personal Data
processed on behalf of the Controller, and delete existing copies
unless UK or EU law requires storage of the Personal Data.

The Service provides a data export mechanism for Controllers to
download a structured export of their practice data prior to
termination. Following termination, Personal Data is retained for
30 days to allow recovery in the event of accidental termination,
then permanently deleted unless an alternative period is agreed.

### 4.9 Audits and inspections

The Processor shall make available to the Controller all information
necessary to demonstrate compliance with the obligations laid down in
Article 28 of the UK GDPR, and allow for and contribute to audits,
including inspections, conducted by the Controller or another auditor
mandated by the Controller, subject to the following:

(a) The Controller shall give at least 30 days' written notice of any
    proposed audit;
(b) Audits shall be conducted no more than once in any twelve-month
    period, except where required to follow up on the findings of an
    earlier audit, or where required by Applicable Data Protection Law
    or by a regulatory authority;
(c) Audits shall be conducted during normal business hours, on dates
    agreed in advance, and in a manner that does not unreasonably
    interfere with the Processor's business operations;
(d) The Controller shall bear its own costs of any audit and shall
    reimburse the Processor's reasonable costs of facilitating the
    audit where the audit demonstrates the Processor is in compliance
    with this DPA. If the audit demonstrates a material breach of this
    DPA by the Processor, the Processor shall bear all such costs.

In lieu of an on-site audit, the Processor may provide the Controller
with copies of relevant certifications, third-party audit reports, or
the Processor's own DSPT submission to demonstrate compliance.

## Schedule 1: Description of Processing

### 1. Subject matter
Personal Data processed by the Processor on behalf of the Controller
in connection with the Controller's use of the GPDash Service.

### 2. Duration
The term of the Principal Agreement, plus the retention periods set
out in Section 6 of this Schedule.

### 3. Nature and purpose
- Receiving and parsing appointment slot data exported from the
  Controller's clinical system (currently EMIS), to compute aggregate
  slot capacity per clinician per session
- Hosting and displaying clinician working patterns and absences
- Hosting and displaying daily rota and buddy-cover allocations
- Syncing absence calendar data from the Controller's TeamNet calendar
- Hosting and displaying aggregated demand-history data uploaded from
  the Controller's online consultation tool
- Providing the user interface through which the Controller's
  authorised staff view and manage the above data

### 4. Types of Personal Data

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Clinician identifiers | Clinician name, initials, role |
| Operational data | Aggregate appointment slot counts per clinician per session per date, working pattern (which weekdays/sessions a clinician works), planned absences (date ranges, reason category) |
| Practice operational metadata | Practice name, ODS code, postcode, list size, configured sites/rooms |

The Service does **not** process patient-identifying data. The
clinical system (EMIS) export consumed by the Service contains only
aggregate slot counts, not patient names or appointment details.

### 5. Categories of Data Subjects
Practice staff:
- Clinicians (GPs, ANPs, nurses, allied health professionals, etc.)
- Administrative and reception staff who appear in working patterns
  or rotas

The Service does not process Personal Data about patients.

### 6. Retention
- Operational data (slot counts, location data per date): rolling
  4-month window (older dates pruned on each upload)
- Working patterns: retained for the life of the clinician's
  registration on the Service
- Planned absences: retained for the life of the clinician's
  registration on the Service
- Aggregate demand history: retained for the life of the Controller's
  account, used to calibrate the demand prediction model
- Audit log entries about Processing of the above: 7 years (NHS
  records-management standard for operational records)

On termination of the Principal Agreement:
- Personal Data is retained for 30 days for accidental-termination
  recovery, then permanently deleted unless the Controller chooses
  to export prior to deletion or to extend the recovery window by
  agreement.

## Schedule 2: Technical and Organisational Measures

The Processor implements the following measures to ensure a level of
security appropriate to the risk:

### 1. Access control
- All access to the Service requires authenticated sign-in via email
  and password
- Multi-factor authentication (TOTP) is enforced for all GPDash
  platform administrators and is available for all Controller users
- Role-based access control within each Controller's practice
  (owner / admin / user) gates editing operations at both the
  application layer and the database layer (row-level security)
- Database access is restricted to GPDash application code via
  service-role credentials held only in production runtime
  environment variables; no human direct access to the production
  database in the normal course of business

### 2. Encryption
- All network traffic between the Controller's browser, the Service,
  and the Service's sub-processors is protected by TLS (HTTPS)
- The Service enforces HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS) with a
  two-year max-age
- Personal Data at rest in the primary database (Supabase Postgres,
  eu-west-2 region) is encrypted using AES-256 at the storage layer

### 3. Logging and monitoring
- The Service maintains an audit log of every operation that modifies
  practice data, recording: actor, action, timestamp, before/after
  values where applicable
- The Service maintains a separate authentication event log recording
  sign-in attempts (success and failure), MFA challenges, password
  changes, and account creation events
- All platform-admin actions are logged separately, with the actor's
  identity and the reason where applicable

### 4. Personnel
- The Processor's current workforce comprises a single individual
  (the founder/operator), who is a UK NHS GP practice administrator
  bound by NHS confidentiality obligations
- Future staff will be required to:
  - Pass appropriate identity and background checks
  - Receive data protection training before being granted access
  - Sign confidentiality agreements as part of their employment terms
- Access to production systems will be limited to staff with a
  documented operational need

### 5. Security headers and application protection
- Content Security Policy restricts what the browser is allowed to
  load, mitigating cross-site scripting
- X-Frame-Options: DENY and CSP frame-ancestors directive prevent
  clickjacking
- Per-endpoint rate limiting (sliding window via Upstash Redis)
  prevents abuse of expensive or sensitive endpoints

### 6. Data minimisation
- The Service deliberately does not request or process patient-
  identifying data
- Retention windows are enforced by a scheduled cleanup job that
  permanently deletes Personal Data past its retention period
  (see `/lib/retention-policy.js`)
- Account holders may export and delete their own Personal Data
  through built-in self-service functions

### 7. Sub-processor controls
- Sub-processors are listed publicly at
  https://gpdash.net/privacy/processors and updated whenever the
  list changes
- Each sub-processor is bound by their own data processing agreement
- The Service has been designed to minimise the data flowing to
  each sub-processor

### 8. Backup and recovery
- Primary database backups are held by Supabase as part of the
  managed service, retained per Supabase's standard policy
- Backups inherit the same geographic restrictions as the primary
  database (UK / EEA)

### 9. Incident response
- A documented breach notification procedure (
  `/docs/legal/breach-notification.md`) governs response to
  suspected or confirmed Personal Data Breaches
- The Processor commits to notifying affected Controllers within
  48 hours of becoming aware of a Breach affecting their Personal
  Data, allowing the Controller to meet its own 72-hour ICO
  notification obligation

### 10. Vulnerability disclosure
- The Processor publishes a security.txt file at
  https://gpdash.net/.well-known/security.txt providing a contact
  for security researchers to report vulnerabilities responsibly

## Schedule 4: Permitted Controller instructions

This Schedule documents the instructions for Processing that the
Controller is taken to have given to the Processor by entering into
this DPA and configuring the Service. The Processor may process
Personal Data only in accordance with these instructions (or other
documented instructions subsequently given by the Controller).

The Controller instructs the Processor to:

1. Receive, parse, and store appointment slot data exported from
   the Controller's clinical system, and present it through the
   Service's user interface to the Controller's authorised users
2. Receive, store, and present working pattern data entered by the
   Controller's authorised users
3. Receive, store, and present planned absence data, either entered
   directly by the Controller's authorised users or synchronised from
   the Controller's TeamNet calendar URL where one has been configured
4. Compute and display derived data (buddy-cover allocations, demand
   predictions, capacity indicators) based on the above
5. Maintain authentication and access control for the Controller's
   authorised users
6. Maintain an audit log of operations performed by the Controller's
   authorised users
7. Apply the retention windows set out in Schedule 1, including
   permanent deletion of Personal Data past its retention period
8. Make Personal Data available to the Controller for export at any
   time during the term of the Principal Agreement, and on termination
9. If the Controller enables the "Public buddy cover page" feature in
   the Service settings, display the buddy cover allocations for the
   Controller's practice at a public URL of the form
   `<gpdash-host>/buddy/<practice-slug>`, accessible without
   authentication, for so long as the Controller leaves the feature
   enabled. The Controller may disable the feature at any time, on
   which the public URL immediately returns a "not found" response.
   The data displayed is limited to: clinicians' names, initials,
   roles, present/absent/day-off status for the day, and cover
   allocations for the day. No patient data is included.
